# MusicTracker — What's New

This file feeds the **"What's New"** widget on the app's home screen.
MusicTracker downloads it straight from GitHub (raw URL), so news can be announced
**without shipping a new build**: just edit this file and push it.

The app fetches a language-specific file — `CHANGELOG.md` (French, the source) or `CHANGELOG.<code>.md`
(e.g. `CHANGELOG.en.md`) — based on the UI language. The base URL is configured in
`MusicTracker/App.config`, key `ChangelogUrl`.

## Expected format

One entry per line: `- <emoji> <text>`.
The first "thing" after the dash is taken as the icon, the rest as the text.
Everything else (headings, blank lines, paragraphs) is **ignored** — so this file stays readable on GitHub.
The most **recent** entries go **first**; the app only shows the first few.

## Entries

- 💾 **Plugin presets, saved at user level**: a "Preset" bar now sits above the editor of any Koton plugin — instrument, effect, generator, note filter. Type a name and hit 💾 to save the settings; recall them from the drop-down, in any piece. The catalogue is global and lives in your user folder, one readable file per preset — so a setting you find one evening is shared by sending a file. No plugin had to be modified to get this: the bar is added by the app, and what it saves (the parameters, plus the internal state for those that have one — a generator's curve, the guqin's string tuning) is what the plugin contract already exposes.
- 🎧 **Hover to hear an instrument**: in a track's instrument menu — and in Koton Live — resting on a plugin's name sounds a held A3, which stops as soon as you leave the entry or click. Going through forty instruments by ear no longer means dropping one on a track, playing a note, and starting over.
- ▶ **Audition an effect from the mixer**: every row of an insert chain gets a button that loops one block of the track — the whole piece on the master — through the chain **up to that effect**. Each row therefore sounds a different state of the signal: the first almost dry, the last the full strip, which lets you judge what one effect adds rather than only what the chain produces as a whole. What you hear is the current settings, including sliders you are moving right now.
- 🎚 **Shared reverb in the mixer**: every strip gets a "Reverb" send feeding **one** common reverb, adjusted from the Master strip. A bus rather than a reverb per track: eight reverberated tracks cost one reverb instead of eight, and the settings don't drift apart the moment you tweak one. The send is taken after the track's inserts and the return enters before the master's, as on a console. Zero by default, so existing pieces sound exactly as before. Not to be confused with a track's old "Reverb" setting, which was only a CC91 sent to the SoundFont and which every Koton plugin and VSTi ignored.
- 🪈 **Armenian duduk**: apricot-wood double-reed pipe, the instrument everyone describes as the closest to the human voice. Formant synthesis — three resonances fixed in absolute frequency, like the vowels of a voice, so they do not follow the note being played. Two settings shape the timbre: **Harmonics**, which says how far up the partial series goes (the duduk's reed is huge and soft, and smothers the top — that is what separates it from the oboe far more than its bore does), and **Odd**, which attenuates the even ranks the way a cylindrical bore closed at the reed end does. A vibrato that **settles in** over a held note instead of being there from the attack, portamento that slides from note to note on slurred phrases, and a continuous breath with the spectrum of real turbulence.

- 🔬 **Life Grid**, a generator you seed rather than program: draw a few cells on a grid, and at every clock step they are born and die under the rules of Conway's Game of Life (or one of eight other rules — HighLife, Maze, Seeds, Replicator…). Rows give pitches (a scale, or the notes of the chord under the block), columns place notes in time, and note lengths aren't set at all: they belong to the cells, a note opening at birth and closing at death. A glider crossing the grid holds its note for the whole crossing, a blinker turns into an ostinato, an R-pentomino develops for dozens of bars before settling. The editor shows both at once — the pattern you drew on the left, its evolution animated on the right — and since the grid almost always ends up dying or freezing, a revive setting re-injects the pattern (or a pinch of cells) when it does.
- 🎼 **Automatable staccato on any track**: a new "Staccato (re-articulation)" curve replays held notes up to 16 times per second. It acts where notes are dispatched, so it works for SoundFont sounds, VSTi and Koton plugins alike — a half note becomes a run of detached eighths, and the curve lets the détaché accelerate through the piece. Along the way, automation now only offers settings that describe a real RANGE: instrument, waveform and mode selectors no longer show up, their values being labels rather than quantities.
- 🎵 **Re-attack** on all 21 physical models: one slider saying how often the note is re-articulated. At 0 it is a held note as before; raise it and the same held note becomes a run of ever shorter ones — tremolo on plucked and struck instruments, tonguing on winds, détaché on bowed strings. The parameter is read continuously, so it automates: a détaché accelerating into flutter over a single held note. Winds and bowed strings do not re-trigger the note (their acoustic loop would take tens of milliseconds to rebuild, and the sound collapsed above 5 strokes per second): the instrument keeps sounding and its output is chopped instead, like a real tongue interrupting the airflow without stopping the tube.
- 🎚 **Plugin parameter automation**: any Koton plugin control can now be driven by a curve along the piece — a violin's vibrato blooming on the held note, a filter sweeping across four bars, a shimmer at 0% in the verse and 60% in the chorus. Right-click a track header → "Add automation": below the usual MIDI controllers, one submenu per plugin on that track (the instrument and each of its effects) listing every parameter. **Master bus** effects automate the same way, from a "Master" row that appears at the top of the timeline as soon as a Koton effect sits there. The curve starts at the value set in the plugin, so adding a lane changes nothing until you drop a point on it; on stop, the original setting is put back.
- 🪈 **Didgeridoo** rebuilt from the measured acoustics of the yidaki: the lip valve now really closes for half of each cycle (that closure is what makes the highs — the old engine died above 700 Hz), the tube is a lip-closed cylinder, so odd harmonics and stretched partials, and the mouth CARVES the spectrum instead of filtering it: the formant you hear is what survives between two notches, exactly as on the real instrument. Keyboard articulations: replaying the same note gives a tongued stroke, or a diaphragm push when played hard; a note an octave up gives a "toot". Circular breathing, tongue flutter and lip drift run on their own, so a single held note is already alive.
- 🔥 **Drive**: a full saturation stage — soft saturation, overdrive, asymmetric tube, distortion, fuzz and wavefolder — with **4× oversampling**. Clipping creates harmonics above half the sample rate, which fold back into the audible band at frequencies unrelated to the note: that is the cheap-digital-distortion sound. Measured here, the oversampling removes 15.6 dB of those artefacts. Bass cut before the gain stage (like a guitar amp), auto-compensated level, parallel mix, 7 presets.
- 🎻 Two effects that follow your pitch instead of detecting notes: **String Resonator** (a Karplus-Strong string excited by your signal, continuously tuned to what you play — or to a fixed note, drone style) and **Synth Violin** (oscillator tuned to your pitch, amplitude driven by your envelope, fixed resonant body). No note is ever decided or quantised: vibrato, glissandi, attacks and dynamics pass through untouched. Usable in the live rack and in the mixer inserts alike.
- 🎤 **Koton Live**, a program of its own: your mic (or MIDI keyboard) in real time. “Effects” mode runs the input through the insert chain (VST2, VST3, Koton plugins, EQ/compressor/delay/saturation) and out to the sound card; “Instrument” mode turns what you play — pitch detected from the mic, just like riff recording, or a MIDI keyboard with velocity, wheels and sustain — into a SoundFont, VSTi or Koton instrument, which then runs through the same effects. WASAPI output everywhere, ASIO for low latency. Launch it on its own (“Koton Live” in the Start menu) or from the 🎤 button in Koton Studio.
- 🎚 VST3 (beta): Koton now also hosts **VST3** plugins — insert effects AND instruments — alongside existing VST2. The scanner picks up `.vst3` (plain files or `Foo.vst3/Contents/x86_64-win/` bundles) from `%CommonProgramFiles%\VST3` and the local `vst\` folder. Automatic routing: `.vst3` → in-house Steinberg P/Invoke host, `.dll` → VST.NET as before. Beta: MIDI CC / pitch-bend / program change are not wired for VST3 instruments yet — notes are enough to try a melodic synth.
- 📁 Portable `vst\` folder: the VST plugin scanner now also inspects a `vst\` folder next to `KotonStudio.exe` (recursive, in addition to the usual Windows paths) — bundle your favourite plugins with a portable install, or try a plugin without installing it system-wide. A local plugin takes precedence over a system plugin with the same name.
- 🎹 VSTi instruments (beta): in a track's header, a new "VSTi instrument…" button replaces the GM MeltySynth patch with a third-party VST2 synth — Vital, Surge XT, TAL-U-No-LX, Spitfire LABS, etc. Menu → filtered list (VSTi only, effects excluded) → click a name; "Edit VSTi…" opens the native GUI, the loaded patch is persisted in the project. The pipeline (mixer, inserts, automation, audio export) is untouched — the VSTi just replaces the sound source. Beta: a crashing plugin can take Koton down, no VST3 or sandboxing yet.
- 🎛 VST plugins (beta): mixer inserts can now host third-party VST2 plugins in addition to the four built-in effects. Menu "+ FX ▸ VST…" scans `%ProgramFiles%\VstPlugins` and `%CommonProgramFiles%\VST2` and lists what it finds; clicking the plugin's name opens its native GUI, and the patch is persisted with your project. Beta: a crashing plugin can take Koton down with it — use at your own risk.
- 🎚 Console-style mixer + per-track insert effects: open the mixer to get a vertical strip per track (fader, pan, mute/solo, level meter) plus a Master strip. Each track (and the master bus) has its own insert chain to fill in as needed: 3-band EQ, Compressor, Delay (with ping-pong) and Saturation. Under the hood, each track now has its own synth and is rendered in parallel — that's what makes per-track processing possible.
- 🎛️ Per-track MIDI automation: right-click a track header → "Add automation" to draw a curve for Pan, Expression, Modulation, Sustain, Reverb, Chorus or Pitch bend alongside the volume lane — click the curve to add points, drag to move, right-click to delete. Double-click a point to reset it to its neutral value.
- 🎼 MusicXML export (Export menu): save your score in the interchange format every notation program reads — Finale, Sibelius, Dorico, MuseScore, online editors and tablet score readers. Notes crossing a barline are now written tied instead of being truncated.
- 🎵 Swing: under "Bar", pick uneven eighths (light, medium, swing, triplet). Playback and audio export follow the groove; the score and MIDI export keep straight eighths.
- 🎶 "Add an instrument / drums with AI" (Track menu): describe an intention; the AI receives the WHOLE piece (every track + the chords) and composes a new track — melodic line (rhythm only) or full melody, or a groove — that fits the existing arrangement.
- 🌍 Language selection (French, English, German, Italian, Spanish, Dutch, Portuguese) in Settings: the interface, the what's-new feed and AI-generated titles all follow the chosen language. More languages can be added by dropping a `lang.xx.json` file.
- 🎲 Generative project templates: a template now holds banks of material per section (progressions, accompaniment patterns, melodic cells, per-instrument phrases, grooves). On opening, the app draws from them and assembles the piece — a "Regenerate" button pulls a new version.
- 🤖 Generate your own styles with AI: describe an intention and the AI builds the template. Right-click a card to regenerate it reusing your intention.
- 🎻 A template's phrases are transposed modally over each chord (with voice leading), and the AI can leave an instrument silent on a section to give the arrangement air.
- 🎹 Much-improved audio rendering: instrument balance fixed (de-duplicated SoundFont modulators and filter-modulator support), no more gap on the piano.
- 🎚️ Velocity-based dynamics: strong beats stand out, off-beats recede — playback breathes instead of being flat.
- 🎶 The AI generates a melodic cell on top of the accompaniment pattern; it is transposed modally over each chord.
- 🏷️ Chord patterns produced by the AI are saved under a name: editable and reusable from the styles picker.
- ↔️ The timeline scrolls continuously during playback, cursor kept centred.
- 🔊 A clear message when no SoundFont is found, at startup as well as on playback.
- 🎛️ Project templates: from a file, with AI, or added to the folder — with deletion.
- 🥁 Catalogue of drum patterns (Standard, Africa, Australia) + your saved, reusable patterns.
- 🔑 Several API keys per provider, chosen by name in the compose screens.
- 🎼 Structured AI templates (intro/theme/development/outro), expanded to the desired length.
- 🎨 Dark & teal interface, movable dialogs, richer editors.
