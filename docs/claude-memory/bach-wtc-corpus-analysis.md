Digested 3 Bach corpora (MIDI from bachcentral.com) through the app's own `MidiImporter.Load` (NAudio → Score, 24 slices/quarter). Files kept at repo `midi\{wtc,art_of_fugue,goldberg}\`; analyzer `analyze_midi.ps1` at repo root (loops over each subfolder; load NAudio.dll then MusicTracker.exe from bin\Debug; reflect over Score.Tracks[].Notes; melodic intervals counted only within MONOPHONIC tracks to avoid voice-jump contamination).

Counts: wtc 61/72 parsed (NAudio rejects 11 file formats), art_of_fugue 18/18, goldberg 32/32.

**Per-work stats (rhythm = note-duration share; motion = melodic intervals, mono voices only):**
- **WTC**: 16th 46% / 8th 28% / 32nd 8% / qtr 7%. Motion 69% step. Voices: preludes 1–2, fugues 3–4. Density 2.4 notes/qtr/voice. Fugue entry stagger ~16 qtr.
- **Art of Fugue (BWV 1080)**: STATELY — 8th 42% / qtr 20% / 16th 13% / half 8% + triplets. Motion 68% step, 9% repeated notes (subject has repeats). Voices mostly **4** (avg 3.4). All tracks monophonic (clean counterpoint). Density only **1.05** (calm). Entry stagger **~43 qtr (~11 bars)** — very wide.
- **Goldberg (BWV 988)**: FLORID/virtuosic — 16th 46% / 8th 22% / **32nd 19%**. Motion 65% step, 31% leap (toccata variations leap across the keyboard). Voices 2–4 (avg 3.1). Density 1.6.
- **English Suites (48 movts, BWV 806-811, from metronimo.com)**: keyboard DANCE suites. 2 tracks = 2 HANDS (mostly polyphonic, not 2-part counterpoint). NEW reliable characteristic = **dance/dotted rhythms**: dotted-8th 8.3% + 8th-triplets 2.1% (the other corpora were ~0-2% dotted) — the courante/gigue/French-overture lilt. Density 2.38 (motoric), 8th 37%/16th 34%/32nd 11%. Melody still ~65% step. Harmony: V→I only 18%, with huge ♭VII/♭VI/♭III approaches — CAVEAT: these long movements MODULATE through related keys, but the analyzer assigns ONE global key, so off-key chords mislabel as chromatic; real takeaway = the big instrumental forms ROAM harmonically (vs the chorale that stays home). A windowed/local-key detector would be needed to read their harmony properly.
- **Solo works (jsbach.net): flute partita BWV 1013 (4), solo violin BWV 1001-1006 (31), solo cello BWV 1007-1012 (36), violin+harpsichord sonatas BWV 1014-1019 (25)** — reveal the BIG one: **IMPLIED POLYPHONY / COMPOUND MELODY**. An UNACCOMPANIED single line LEAPS far MORE than an accompanied one, because it must arpeggiate to imply the harmony: flute (truly monophonic) = **53% leaps** (step only 46%), 85% sixteenths (moto perpetuo); solo violin 41% leaps; solo cello 42% leaps. BUT the violin+harpsichord sonatas (harmony provided by the keyboard) = only **37% leaps / 59% step** — the violin sings stepwise once it no longer carries the harmony. → REFINES the "~69% stepwise" universal: that holds for ACCOMPANIED/polyphonic textures; a SOLO lead line deliberately leaps/arpeggiates (compound melody). Also: violin sonatas render as **3 voices** (violin + 2 harpsichord lines = TRIO SONATA); solo violin/cello render as 3-4 "voices" = the implied polyphony of double-stops/chords. Actionable for the composer: a solo/unaccompanied lead should arpeggiate chord tones (more leaps); an accompanied melody should be stepwise.
- **Chorales (15, harmonized hymns)**: HOMOPHONIC/CHORDAL — the OPPOSITE texture. Always **exactly 4 voices** (SATB), all entering TOGETHER (entry stagger = 0, no imitation), **homorhythmic** slow values (quarter 58% / 8th 30% / half 9%, almost no 16ths, density ~1). Densest, most explicit FUNCTIONAL HARMONY of all: **V→I = 51% of tonic approaches and 60% of closes**, trigrams I-V-I/IV-V-I/ii-V-I, strong T-S-D-T flow. Melody still ~65% step but 14% repeated notes (syllabic hymn tune). → a 4th distinct Bach characteristic: chordal note-against-note harmonization, not figuration/counterpoint.

**Cross-work conclusions (the actionable ones):**
1. **There is no single "Bach rhythm."** Stately fugue (8th+qtr, density~1) vs. balanced WTC (16th+8th, density~2.4) vs. florid Goldberg (16th + heavy 32nd). → the composer's Bach rhythm should be a SELECTABLE sub-profile, not one palette.
2. **Stepwise motion is the universal constant (~65–69%)** across all three, leaps almost always ≤ P5 and dominated by m3. → strong stepwise bias + cap leaps at the fifth is correct for ANY Bach style. THE most robust rule.
3. **Counterpoint = 3–4 voices, monophonic, widely-staggered entries** (AoF: 4 voices, entries ~11 bars apart). My CanonMelody uses a 1-bar delay → should stagger by ~subject length and default to 3 voices.
4. **Repeated notes matter** in subjects (AoF 9%); my generator avoids repeats — allowing some would help.
5. Density is a style dial: ~1 (calm AoF) → 2.4 (WTC) → fast-values (Goldberg).

**Harmonic schema (analyze_harmony.ps1: Krumhansl-Schmuckler key detection — self-validates: AoF 18/18 Dm, Goldberg 28M/4m, WTC 33/28 — then per-beat triad template-match → Roman-numeral degree relative to tonic; dropped the augmented template, it over-matched dominants/passing tones):**
- **Universal cadential formula = functional T → S → D → T**, realized as **(ii or IV) → V → I**. ii-V-I and IV-V-I are top trigrams in ALL three works.
- **Three cadence types coexist**: authentic **V→I** (primary; closes 23% WTC, approach-to-I 33% Goldberg), plagal **IV→I / borrowed iv→I** (strong 2nd; 16% WTC closes, 28% AoF Picardy closes), leading-tone **vii°→I** (~7–10%).
- **I–IV–V–I backbone**: I→IV, IV→V, V→I are top transitions everywhere; pre-dominant always precedes the dominant.
- **Deceptive + tonic substitutes**: V→vi, vi→IV, vi→I (vi/iii prolong the tonic).
- **Secondary dominants**: chromatic majors on minor degrees (e.g. WTC "II→V" = V/V).
- **Minor mode is modal+chromatic**: heavy **bVII↔i** (Aeolian subtonic), bIII (relative major), BOTH minor-v and major-V, and **Picardy-third endings** (minor pieces close on major I — detector reads final i as I).
- Per-work color: WTC balanced; Goldberg very tonal (V→I 33%); Art of Fugue modal-minor (bVII↔i dominates, iv→I Picardy closes).
→ Composer cadence generator (MusicTheory.Cadence, functional) is VALIDATED; refine weights toward (ii/IV)→V→I, add minor-mode bVII/modal + Picardy option + deceptive V→vi.

**Pattern analysis (analyze_patterns.ps1: rhythm 3-gram cells, contour direction 3-grams + run length, melodic motif/sequence repetition; mono tracks):**
- **Rhythmic Fortspinnung**: the top rhythm cell is almost always THREE OF THE SAME value (8-8-8, 16-16-16, q-q-q, 32-32-32). Bach picks a prevailing pulse for a passage and stays on it. Dominant value is genre-specific: chorales q-q-q (29%), Art of Fugue 8-8-8 (25%), WTC/cello 16-16-16, Goldberg 16+32, English Suites 8-8-8 (46%!) + dotted cells (8.-8.-8.). Per-piece self-similarity (share of the piece's #1 cell): suites/cello ~59%, Goldberg 42%, WTC 40%, chorales/AoF ~30%. → figurative pieces are rhythmically UNIFORM (one cell pervades). My BarRhythm re-randomises every beat = too patchwork; should bias to ONE dominant value/piece + occasional variation + longer notes at cadences.
- **Contour**: avg monotonic RUN LENGTH ≈ **1.8–2.0 steps before turning** across ALL corpora — Bach changes direction ~every 2 notes (no long ramps). Figuration favours zigzag DUD/UDU (~13-16%, the implied-polyphony alternation); chorales/fugues more arch-like/balanced. The 6 contour 3-grams are otherwise fairly even.
- **Melodic SEQUENCE / motif repetition is HIGH** (% notes in a within-piece repeated interval-3gram): English Suites **96%**, Goldberg 78%, Art of Fugue 71%, cello 67%, WTC 66%, chorales 55%. → Bach is intensely MOTIVIC: a short cell spun out, repeated transposed (a sequence). My current wandering-walk melody is NON-motivic — misses this. The fix: generate a short balanced motif (run~2) and repeat it transposed along the harmony, VARYING the sequence direction (so it's motivic like Bach WITHOUT the old sawtooth).

**Rest / breathing analysis (analyze_rests.ps1: gaps between a mono voice's note-end and next onset):** Chorales & flute partita ~0% internal silence (sustained / moto perpetuo — breathing only between phrases); WTC ~15%, Art of Fugue ~13% with gaps that are real breaths (≥ quarter, landing on beats — voices drop out and re-enter = the breathing of counterpoint); solo cello/violin ~50% PER rendered voice (the implied-polyphony alternation, an artifact of multi-track rendering). Silence isn't filler — it's the articulation/phrasing.

**Counterpoint analysis (analyze_counterpoint.ps1: harmonic rhythm, bass, outer voices, suspensions; samples full polyphony per beat):** for the SOLO+accompaniment target (violin+harpsichord sonatas): **harmonic rhythm ≈ 3 chord changes/bar** (my composer was 1/bar = far too static — the #1 gap), **bass 39% stepwise / 61% root leaps**, **outer voices (top vs bass) 28% 3rds + 17% 6ths = ~45% imperfect consonances, 35% contrary motion**, **suspensions ~6-7% of beats**. Chorales: harmonic rhythm 3/bar, 44% contrary, low dissonance.
